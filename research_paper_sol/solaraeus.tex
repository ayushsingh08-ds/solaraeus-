\begin{abstract}
Pedestrian thermal stress poses an escalating public health crisis in rapidly densifying tropical metropolises, yet existing microclimate modeling packages operate as monolithic, desktop-bound batch solvers. When localized architectural interventions (e.g., shade canopies or tree plantings) are evaluated, traditional engines discard domain state and recompute all spatial fields from scratch, creating a computational bottleneck that precludes real-time interactive design and automated optimization. In this paper, we present \textbf{Solaraeus}, an end-to-end, physics-based 3D urban microclimate digital twin and high-throughput computational engine. Solaraeus harmonizes open geospatial datasets---Overture building vector polygons, Google Open Buildings machine-learning height estimates, FABDEM topography, and NOAA/NASA POWER meteorological fluxes---into an automated Python and CUDA C++ simulation pipeline. The core engine executes astronomical solar vector tracking, Steyn (1980) Sky View Factor (SVF) calculation across 36 azimuth radials, 3D Möller--Trumbore ray-traced directional shadows, 6-flux Stefan--Boltzmann Mean Radiant Temperature ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$) balance, and Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) biometeorological stress evaluation. To overcome the recomputation bottleneck, we formulate and prove a \textit{Certified Incremental Solver} with closed-form error bounds $B_T(x) \le \varepsilon_T = 0.50\,\mathrm{K}$ grounded in concave Stefan--Boltzmann linearization and solid-angle horizon truncation decay. We couple this with a resident-memory CUDA GPU acceleration engine and an active surrogate optimizer for multi-intervention shade layout search.

Solaraeus is evaluated on an empirical real-world digital twin of the Church Street pedestrian corridor in Bengaluru, India ($380\,\mathrm{m} \times 296\,\mathrm{m}$ simulation domain, $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ grid, $28{,}120$ cells, 123 buildings). Baseline simulations reveal severe pedestrian heat stress during peak pre-monsoon conditions ($T_a = 35.0\degC$, $\mathrm{GHI} = 755.97\,\Wm$), with corridor $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ averaging $45.81\degC$ and UTCI averaging $36.49\degC$ (Strong Heat Stress), contrasting against building-shaded zones by $\Delta T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 11.89\,\mathrm{K}$. Introducing an overhead shade canopy ($\mathrm{BLR\_SHADE\_001}$, $6\,\mathrm{m} \times 3\,\mathrm{m}$) delivers a peak localized cooling relief of $-12.62\,\mathrm{K}$ $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ and $-3.10\,\mathrm{K}$ UTCI. The certified incremental solver recomputes only $72$ cells ($0.26\%$) while reusing $28{,}048$ cells ($99.74\%$), avoiding $925{,}584$ rays ($99.74\%$ work reduction) with a maximum observed domain error of $0.0289\,\mathrm{K} \ll 0.50\,\mathrm{K}$ and zero certificate violations across 18 audited criteria. On an NVIDIA RTX 4050 GPU, the resident incremental kernel executes in $11.40\,\mathrm{ms}$ ($500.87\times$ speedup over full CPU recomputation; $36.84\times$ over CPU incremental). Multi-intervention surrogate optimization discovers a complementary dual-panel configuration ($\mathrm{CAND\_4196\_SURR}$, $22.87\,\mathrm{m}^2$) delivering $12.68\,\mathrm{K}$ peak cooling with zero spatial overlap. Outputs are serialized into typed JSON contracts and ingested by an interactive Three.js 3D WebGL digital twin with custom GLSL thermal shaders.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Accelerating urbanization compounded by climate-induced heatwaves has intensified the Urban Heat Island (UHI) phenomenon, subjecting metropolitan populations to dangerous levels of outdoor physiological strain \cite{oke1982canyon,grimmond2010climate}. In dense equatorial and tropical cities such as Bengaluru, India ($12.97^\circ\,\mathrm{N}$), high solar elevation angles and deep concrete-and-asphalt street canyons generate extreme diurnal thermal loads. Within pedestrian corridors, daytime physiological comfort is dictated predominantly by the Mean Radiant Temperature ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$) rather than dry-bulb ambient air temperature ($T_a$) alone \cite{matzarakis2007modelling}. $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ integrates shortwave solar irradiance (direct beam, diffuse sky, and surface reflections) and longwave emissions from heated building facades and ground pavements incident upon the human body. Under direct tropical sun, radiant heat flux can elevate pedestrian thermal perception into the \textit{Strong} and \textit{Very Strong Heat Stress} classifications of the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) \cite{jendritzky2012universal,brode2012deriving}, inducing heat exhaustion and cardiovascular stress.

Consequently, municipal urban planners and landscape architects require high-resolution microclimatic models to evaluate mitigation strategies, including engineered shade structures, street canopies, and tree plantings \cite{chen2012gis,chatzipoulka2016sky}. To model these phenomena, established microclimate engines have been deployed, most notably the Solar and Longwave Environmental Irradiance Geometry (SOLWEIG) model within the Urban Multi-scale Environmental Predictor (UMEP) \cite{lindberg2008solweig,lindberg2018urban}, microscale Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) suites like ENVI-met \cite{bruse1998simulating}, and Radiance-based CAD plugins like Ladybug Tools \cite{sadeghipour2013ladybug}. While CFD suites resolve localized convective air turbulence, convective heat exchange accounts for less than one-third of pedestrian daytime thermal sensation in clear-sky conditions compared to radiation \cite{matzarakis2007modelling}. SOLWEIG establishes an efficient trade-off by decoupling 3D geometric radiative balance from turbulent flow, using discrete ray casting across Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) to derive Sky View Factors (SVF) and cast shadows \cite{gal2009sky,lindberg2010spatial}.

Despite their theoretical maturity, current microclimate modeling workflows suffer from three fundamental computational and operational bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Monolithic Recomputation Bottleneck:} When an urban designer introduces a localized architectural alteration---such as placing an overhead awning or planting a shade tree---existing solvers discard the entire simulation domain and recalculate all cells from scratch. Because city-scale grids contain tens of thousands to millions of cells with $36$ to $360$ rays per cell, this exhaustive recalculation consumes seconds to minutes per iteration, rendering real-time design exploration and automated algorithmic optimization infeasible.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Formal Error Certificates:} While heuristic spatial clipping or bounding boxes can restrict recomputation, ad-hoc approximations risk unquantified error propagation into surrounding radiative fields. No existing urban microclimate tool provides a mathematically proven, closed-form error certificate guaranteeing that untouched cells do not deviate beyond a user-specified thermal tolerance ($|\widetilde{T}_{\mathrm{mrt}}(x) - T_{\mathrm{mrt}}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)| \le \varepsilon_T$).
    \item \textbf{Lack of Hardware Acceleration and Interactive 3D Web Integration:} Existing scientific software packages remain desktop-bound and CPU-constrained, exporting static GIS shapefiles or 2D raster imagery rather than publishing open, typed data contracts to hardware-accelerated 3D WebGL runtimes \cite{sadeghipour2013ladybug,lindberg2018urban}.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these barriers, this paper presents \textbf{Solaraeus}, an end-to-end, physics-based 3D urban microclimate digital twin and certified high-throughput computational engine. The primary scientific and engineering contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Fidelity Geospatial Harmonization:} An automated ingestion pipeline that fuses Overture Maps vector building perimeters, Google Open Buildings machine-learning height estimates, FABDEM topography, and NOAA/NASA POWER meteorological forcing into watertight 3D meshes and metric grids in Bengaluru, India.
    \item \textbf{Certified Incremental Solver Formulation:} A mathematically proved recomputation algorithm that evaluates closed-form error bounds $B_T(x) \le \varepsilon_T$ based on solid-angle horizon elevation decay and concave Stefan--Boltzmann linearization, provably guaranteeing bounded error while reusing over $99.7\%$ of static grid cells.
    \item \textbf{Resident-Memory CUDA GPU Engine:} High-throughput CUDA C++ kernels implementing double-precision Möller--Trumbore ray tracing and horizon SVF marching on NVIDIA GPUs, achieving bit-identical shadow parity, machine-precision $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ parity, and $11.40\,\mathrm{ms}$ incremental updates ($500.87\times$ speedup over full CPU recomputation).
    \item \textbf{Active Surrogate Optimization:} A constrained multi-intervention layout optimizer that searches single-panel and multi-panel shade configurations under geometric canyon clearance rules and shadow overlap interference penalties, identifying Pareto-optimal cooling interventions.
    \item \textbf{Interactive 3D WebGL Digital Twin:} A production Three.js r128 web digital twin that ingests typed JSON data contracts, rendering interactive physical layers (DSM, SVF, shadows, $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$, UTCI), real-time sun tracking, and a pedestrian comfort HUD.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section~II reviews related literature. Section~III describes the Church Street study site and geospatial harmonization. Section~IV details the governing physics engine. Section~V formalizes the certified incremental solver and mathematical error bounds. Section~VI details the CUDA GPU acceleration architecture. Section~VII presents the active surrogate optimization framework. Section~VIII reports comprehensive empirical baseline and intervention results. Section~IX concludes the paper.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Outdoor Thermal Comfort and Mean Radiant Temperature}
Pedestrian thermal sensation in urban outdoor spaces has been extensively investigated \cite{oke1982canyon,grimmond2010climate}. Fanger's seminal work \cite{fanger1970thermal} established the fundamental heat exchange balance between the human body and the surrounding thermal environment. Outdoor biometeorological research subsequently formalized indices such as the Physiological Equivalent Temperature (PET) \cite{matzarakis2007modelling} and the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) \cite{jendritzky2012universal,brode2012deriving}. UTCI couples a multi-node dynamic human thermoregulation model (Fiala et al.\ \cite{fiala2012utci}) with an adaptive clothing insulation algorithm. Across diverse urban canyon morphologies, $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ represents the single most influential meteorological driver of outdoor heat stress: daytime fluctuations in shortwave solar and longwave terrestrial radiant fluxes impact pedestrian physiological strain more than twice as intensely as equivalent variations in ambient air temperature \cite{matzarakis2007modelling,brode2012deriving}.

\subsection{Radiative Transfer Modeling and SOLWEIG}
Simulating outdoor radiative transfer requires solving complex line-of-sight occlusions across 3D urban geometries \cite{robinson2004sun,krayenhoff2014multi}. A foundational metric is the Sky View Factor (SVF), defined as the ratio of visible radiant sky hemisphere unobstructed by surrounding built surfaces \cite{chen2012gis,chatzipoulka2016sky}. Steyn \cite{steyn1980svf} introduced the angular ray-marching method across radial azimuths over digital elevation grids. Lindberg et al.\ extended this concept in the SOLWEIG model \cite{lindberg2008solweig,lindberg2010spatial}, modeling direct beam, diffuse sky irradiance, and reflected longwave emissions across six cardinal orthogonal directions (upward, downward, north, south, east, and west) on Fanger's rotationally symmetric human cylinder. While SOLWEIG dramatically reduces runtime relative to Navier--Stokes CFD suites, its implementation in UMEP relies on raster-based Python/QGIS batch processing, where any spatial scene edit requires recomputing the entire raster domain \cite{lindberg2018urban}.

\subsection{GPU Ray Tracing and Incremental Spatial Computation}
In computer graphics and computational geometry, hardware-accelerated ray tracing and spatial caching (e.g., Bounding Volume Hierarchies, shadow map caching, and temporal reprojection) are standard techniques for avoiding redundant work across interactive updates \cite{wald2014embree,parker2010optix}. In urban environmental simulation, researchers have explored GPU acceleration to speed up solar irradiance calculations \cite{bauer2020accelerating,zhang2019gpu,kastner2020ray}. However, existing GPU implementations remain strictly batch-oriented: when a localized geometric intervention (e.g., shade canopy or tree) is modified, the solver recomputes the entire scene from scratch. While incremental visibility techniques have been studied conceptually in spatial computing \cite{teller1992visibility}, their rigorous mathematical coupling with certified thermal error bounds and multi-directional SOLWEIG-compatible flux integration has not been realized in urban microclimate science.

\subsection{Surrogate-Assisted Spatial Optimization}
Optimizing microclimatic interventions across complex urban canyons is intrinsically challenging due to non-convex physical objective landscapes, geometric canyon boundaries, and significant evaluation latency \cite{forrester2008engineering}. Surrogate-assisted optimization replaces computationally expensive simulation evaluations with statistical response surfaces (e.g., Gaussian processes, radial basis functions, or polynomial models) guided by exploration-exploitation acquisition functions \cite{forrester2008engineering}. Applying active surrogate modeling to certified incremental microclimate simulations offers unprecedented potential for multi-intervention urban design.

\section{Study Site and Geospatial Data Harmonization}

\subsection{Church Street, Bengaluru Study Site}
To evaluate Solaraeus on an authentic, high-density tropical urban corridor, we selected Church Street in central Bengaluru (Karnataka, India; $12.974900^\circ\,\mathrm{N}$, $77.605400^\circ\,\mathrm{E}$). Church Street is a flagship pedestrian-priority commercial avenue characterized by continuous multi-story retail and office facades, dense foot traffic, and limited natural tree shade. During the pre-monsoon summer season (March to May), Bengaluru experiences acute daytime heat with clear-sky solar irradiances exceeding $750\,\Wm$ and ambient temperatures surpassing $35^\circ\mathrm{C}$, creating severe pedestrian heat stress.

The simulation domain is partitioned into:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Core Study Block:} A $218.46\,\mathrm{m} \times 135.05\,\mathrm{m}$ bounding area ($28{,}840.88\,\mathrm{m}^2$) enclosing 37 mapped core building footprints along the primary pedestrian promenade.
    \item \textbf{Shadow Context Domain:} The core site expanded outward by a $75\,\mathrm{m}$ metric buffer to capture incoming building shadow rays cast by external high-rises at oblique solar angles. The resulting domain measures $380.0\,\mathrm{m} \times 296.0\,\mathrm{m}$, discretized onto a uniform $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ grid ($190 \times 148 = 28{,}120$ cells).
    \item \textbf{Pedestrian Corridor:} A 12-metre-wide corridor centered along Church Street containing $N = 668$ active pedestrian ground cells reserved for detailed comfort auditing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Coordinate System Harmonization}
Geospatial inputs originate from disparate geographic and projected coordinate reference systems (CRS). Solaraeus harmonizes all spatial layers into the Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) Zone 43N conformal projection (EPSG:32643, WGS~84 datum). The local simulation coordinate origin $(0, 0)$ is pinned to:
\begin{equation}
    X_{\mathrm{origin}} = 782{,}541.81\,\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{E}, \quad Y_{\mathrm{origin}} = 1{,}435{,}736.11\,\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{equation}
Because the street corridor is oriented obliquely to True North, the local grid convergence angle is strictly computed as $\gamma = +0.5854^\circ$. All solar azimuth calculations are rotated by $\gamma$ to align true astronomical vectors with metric grid axes without spatial distortion.

\subsection{Dual-Taxonomy Building Mesh Construction}
Building structural envelopes are synthesized using a dual-taxonomy dataset reconciling open Overture Maps vector polygons with Google Open Buildings machine-learning footprints. The scene integrates 123 buildings (37 core, 86 context). To address height uncertainties, building heights are categorized into:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Direct Height Metadata:} 7 buildings possessing municipal floor counts or recorded lidar height attributes ($H_k \in [8.0\,\mathrm{m}, 35.0\,\mathrm{m}]$).
    \item \textit{ML Inferred Heights:} 116 buildings assigned median building heights derived from Google Open Buildings ML confidence rasters ($H_k \in [6.5\,\mathrm{m}, 28.0\,\mathrm{m}]$).
\end{itemize}
All building polygons are triangulated into watertight 3D meshes consisting of $2{,}136$ triangles, with planar roofs, vertical facade quads, and validated 2-manifold topology. Ground terrain is established at datum $z = 0.0\,\mathrm{m}$ (with synthetic and FABDEM topographic extensions evaluated under documented protocols).

\subsection{Atmospheric and Solar Forcing Baseline}
Atmospheric forcing conditions were acquired for the extreme pre-monsoon heatwave of April 15, 2024 at 09:00 UTC (14:30 Indian Standard Time, IST). Boundary meteorology is derived from the NOAA Integrated Surface Database (ISD) Bengaluru City Station 43295099999 ($2.56\,\mathrm{km}$ southwest of Church Street):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Air temperature: $T_a = 35.0\degC$ ($308.15\,\mathrm{K}$),
    \item Relative humidity: $\mathrm{RH} = 19.73\%$ (dewpoint $T_d = 8.9\degC$),
    \item Wind velocity: $v_{10} = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $90^\circ$ (easterly), scaled to pedestrian height ($1.1\,\mathrm{m}$) via the urban logarithmic wind law as $v_{1.1} = 1.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
\end{itemize}
Solar irradiances are obtained from NASA POWER hourly satellite reanalysis: Global Horizontal Irradiance $\mathrm{GHI} = 755.97\,\Wm$, Direct Normal Irradiance $\mathrm{DNI} = 728.31\,\Wm$, and Diffuse Horizontal Irradiance $\mathrm{DHI} = 172.18\,\Wm$. NOAA astronomical solar position algorithms evaluate the sun position at altitude $\alpha_s = 57.9160^\circ$ and True North azimuth $\phi_s = 268.1655^\circ$ (Grid North azimuth $\phi_{\mathrm{grid}} = 267.5802^\circ$). Physical properties are assigned standard literature values: building wall albedo $\alpha_{\mathrm{bldg}} = 0.20$, ground asphalt albedo $\alpha_{\mathrm{gnd}} = 0.15$, and building wall emissivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bldg}} = 0.90$ (summarized in Table~\ref{tab:inputs}).

\section{Physics Engine and Radiative Transfer}

\subsection{Sky View Factor (SVF) Ray-Marching}
The Sky View Factor $\Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}(x) \in [0, 1]$ at pedestrian eye level ($z_{\mathrm{ped}} = 1.1\,\mathrm{m}$) measures the fraction of unobstructed sky hemisphere seen from location $x = (u, v)$. Solaraeus calculates $\Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}$ using the Steyn \cite{steyn1980svf} angular ray-marching formulation across $N_\phi = 36$ azimuth radials ($\Delta \phi = 10^\circ$):
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}(u,v) = \frac{1}{N_{\phi}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\phi}} \bigl( 1 - \sin \beta(u,v,\phi_j) \bigr),
    \label{eq:svf_steyn}
\end{equation}
where $\beta(u,v,\phi_j)$ is the maximum horizon obstacle elevation angle subtended along azimuth radial $\phi_j$ within radial cutoff radius $R_{\max} = 150\,\mathrm{m}$:
\begin{equation}
    \tan \beta(u,v,\phi_j) = \max_{r \in (0, R_{\max}]} \frac{z_{\mathrm{mesh}}(u + r\cos\phi_j, v + r\sin\phi_j) - z_{\mathrm{ped}}}{r}.
    \label{eq:horizon}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Möller--Trumbore Directional Ray Tracing}
Ground-level shadow occlusions are resolved by casting directional rays from each pedestrian sensor $(u, v, z_{\mathrm{ped}})$ toward the unit solar vector $\vec{s} = (\cos\alpha_s \sin\phi_{\mathrm{grid}},\; \cos\alpha_s \cos\phi_{\mathrm{grid}},\; \sin\alpha_s)$. Solaraeus implements the double-precision Möller--Trumbore ray-triangle intersection algorithm \cite{moller1997fast}. For each candidate triangle with vertices $\vec{V}_0, \vec{V}_1, \vec{V}_2$, the ray-plane intersection is parameterized via barycentric coordinates $(t, u_b, v_b)$:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix} t \\ u_b \\ v_b \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{\vec{P} \cdot \vec{E}_1} \begin{bmatrix} \vec{Q} \cdot \vec{E}_2 \\ \vec{P} \cdot \vec{T} \\ \vec{Q} \cdot \vec{D} \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eq:moller_trumbore}
\end{equation}
where $\vec{E}_1 = \vec{V}_1 - \vec{V}_0$, $\vec{E}_2 = \vec{V}_2 - \vec{V}_0$, $\vec{T} = \vec{O} - \vec{V}_0$, $\vec{P} = \vec{D} \times \vec{E}_2$, and $\vec{Q} = \vec{T} \times \vec{E}_1$. A valid intersection occurs if $t > 0$, $u_b \ge 0$, $v_b \ge 0$, and $u_b + v_b \le 1$. The resulting direct solar beam availability mask is binary:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}(u,v) = \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if ray intersects any building or panel,} \\ 1, & \text{if ray reaches the unobstructed sky.} \end{cases}
    \label{eq:shadow_def}
\end{equation}

\subsection{6-Flux Radiant Energy Balance and $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$}
Total radiant energy absorbed by a human pedestrian is evaluated across six orthogonal spatial directions (upward, downward, north, south, east, and west) based on Fanger's cylindrical human geometry \cite{fanger1970thermal,lindberg2008solweig}:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\mathrm{tot}}(u,v) = \alpha_p \sum_{i=1}^6 F_i K_i(u,v) + \varepsilon_p \sum_{i=1}^6 F_i L_i(u,v),
    \label{eq:flux_sum}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_p = 0.70$ is the human shortwave absorption coefficient, $\varepsilon_p = 0.97$ is the longwave skin/clothing emissivity, and $F_i$ are cylindrical form factors ($F_{\mathrm{up}} = F_{\mathrm{down}} = 0.06$, $F_{\mathrm{north}} = F_{\mathrm{south}} = F_{\mathrm{east}} = F_{\mathrm{west}} = 0.22$, satisfying $\sum F_i = 1.0$).

Shortwave fluxes partition into direct, diffuse, and reflected components:
\begin{align}
    K_{\mathrm{dir}}(u,v) &= I_{\mathrm{dir}} \mathcal{S}(u,v), \label{eq:kdir} \\
    K_{\mathrm{diff}}(u,v) &= I_{\mathrm{diff}} \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}(u,v), \label{eq:kdiff} \\
    K_{\mathrm{refl}}(u,v) &= (1 - \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}})\alpha_{\mathrm{bldg}} I_{\mathrm{glob}} + \alpha_{\mathrm{gnd}} I_{\mathrm{glob}}. \label{eq:krefl}
\end{align}
Longwave fluxes integrate downward atmospheric counter-radiation $L_{\mathrm{sky}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{sky}}\sigma T_a^4$ and emissions from building walls ($T_{\mathrm{bldg}} \approx T_a + 2\,\mathrm{K}$) and ground surfaces ($T_{\mathrm{gnd}} \approx T_a + 7\,\mathrm{K}$):
\begin{equation}
    L_{\mathrm{down}}(u,v) = \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}} L_{\mathrm{sky}} + (1 - \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}) \varepsilon_{\mathrm{bldg}} \sigma T_{\mathrm{bldg}}^4.
    \label{eq:ldown}
\end{equation}
The Mean Radiant Temperature ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ in Kelvin) is obtained via the Stefan--Boltzmann fourth-root inversion:
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm{mrt}}(u,v) = \left( \frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{tot}}(u,v)}{\sigma} \right)^{1/4},
    \label{eq:tmrt}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma = 5.67037 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W/(m^2\,K^4)}$.

\subsection{Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI)}
Pedestrian physiological heat stress is quantified via the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) \cite{jendritzky2012universal,brode2012deriving} executed via the certified \texttt{pythermalcomfort} library:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{UTCI}(u,v) = f_{\mathrm{poly}}\bigl(T_a, T_{\mathrm{mrt}}(u,v), v_{1.1}, \mathrm{RH}\bigr),
    \label{eq:utci}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\mathrm{poly}}$ is an operational 6th-order polynomial approximation of the 187-node dynamic Fiala human thermoregulation model.

\section{Certified Incremental Radiative Transfer}

\subsection{Theoretical Error Bound and Certificate Theorem}
When an urban intervention (e.g., an overhead shade canopy $\mathcal{P}$) is introduced, existing tools recompute all $N_{\mathrm{grid}}$ cells. Solaraeus partitions the domain into a dirty recomputation subset $\Omega_{\mathrm{dirty}}$ and an invariant clean subset $\Omega_{\mathrm{clean}} = \Omega \setminus \Omega_{\mathrm{dirty}}$. To guarantee physical soundness, we formalize the error certificate:

\begin{definition}[Thermal Error Certificate]
Let $\widetilde{T}_{\mathrm{mrt}}(x)$ denote the temperature obtained by reusing static baseline fields at $x \in \Omega_{\mathrm{clean}}$, and $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)$ denote the full recomputation temperature. An update is \textbf{certified safe} with tolerance $\varepsilon_T$ if:
\begin{equation}
    |\widetilde{T}_{\mathrm{mrt}}(x) - T_{\mathrm{mrt}}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)| \le B_T(x) \le \varepsilon_T, \quad \forall x \in \Omega_{\mathrm{clean}}.
    \label{eq:cert_def}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closed-Form Stefan--Boltzmann Bound]
Let $\Delta \Phi_{\max}(x)$ be an upper bound on radiant flux perturbation at cell $x$ caused by intervention $\mathcal{P}$, and let $T_{\min}$ be the minimum physically plausible absolute temperature ($T_{\min} \ge 273.15\,\mathrm{K}$). Then:
\begin{equation}
    B_T(x) = \frac{\Delta \Phi_{\max}(x)}{4 \sigma T_{\min}^3} \le \varepsilon_T.
    \label{eq:thm_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ denote the baseline and intervention radiant fluxes at $x$, with $\Phi_2 = \Phi_1 + \Delta \Phi$. By the Mean Value Theorem applied to $g(\Phi) = (\Phi/\sigma)^{1/4}$:
\begin{equation}
    |T_{\mathrm{mrt}, 2} - T_{\mathrm{mrt}, 1}| = |g(\Phi_2) - g(\Phi_1)| = \frac{|\Delta \Phi|}{4 \sigma^{1/4} \xi^{3/4}},
\end{equation}
for some $\xi \in [\min(\Phi_1, \Phi_2), \max(\Phi_1, \Phi_2)]$. Since $\xi = \sigma T_\xi^4 \ge \sigma T_{\min}^4$, we have $\xi^{3/4} \ge \sigma^{3/4} T_{\min}^3$. Substituting yields:
\begin{equation}
    |T_{\mathrm{mrt}, 2} - T_{\mathrm{mrt}, 1}| \le \frac{|\Delta \Phi_{\max}|}{4 \sigma T_{\min}^3}.
\end{equation}
By setting $\Delta \Phi_{\max}(x) \le 4 \sigma T_{\min}^3 \varepsilon_T$, the bound is strictly satisfied.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bounding Flux Perturbation and Affected Regions}
The flux perturbation $\Delta \Phi_{\max}(x)$ combines direct shadow ray occlusion and solid-angle SVF reduction:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \Phi_{\max}(x) = \alpha_p I_{\mathrm{dir}} \Delta \mathcal{S}(x) + \Delta \Phi_{\mathrm{diff,refl}}(x),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta \mathcal{S}(x) = 1$ if cell $x$ lies in the shadow frustum cast by canopy $\mathcal{P}$ along sun vector $\vec{s}$, and $\Delta \mathcal{S}(x) = 0$ otherwise. For all cells outside the geometric shadow frustum, $\Delta \mathcal{S}(x) = 0$. The diffuse and longwave perturbations decay quadratically with distance $r(x) = \mathrm{dist}(x, \mathcal{P})$ according to the solid-angle horizon cutoff:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}(x) \le \min\left(1.0, \frac{W_{\mathcal{P}} H_{\mathcal{P}}}{2\pi r(x)^2}\right),
    \label{eq:svf_decay}
\end{equation}
where $W_{\mathcal{P}}$ and $H_{\mathcal{P}}$ are the projected width and height of the canopy. Cells where $B_T(x) > \varepsilon_T$ or $\Delta \mathcal{S}(x) = 1$ are assigned to $\Omega_{\mathrm{dirty}}$ and recomputed; all remaining cells retain baseline values with certified zero error.

\section{CUDA GPU Acceleration Architecture}

To achieve real-time simulation throughput, Solaraeus integrates a custom CUDA C++ acceleration engine via CuPy \cite{bauer2020accelerating}. The GPU engine resolves two critical bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Resident VRAM State:} Mesh geometry (vertices, normal vectors, triangle face indices), ground elevation arrays, and sensor coordinates reside permanently in GPU global memory. When candidate interventions are updated, only the incremental triangle buffers are streamed to VRAM, eliminating host-to-device PCI-e ping-pong transfer overhead.
    \item \textbf{Selective Ray Dispatch:} Rather than launching rays across all $28{,}120$ cells, the GPU incremental kernel dispatches CUDA thread blocks strictly over the affected sensor subset $\Omega_{\mathrm{dirty}}$.
\end{enumerate}

The GPU pipeline executes two specialized CUDA kernels:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{moller\_trumbore\_shadow\_kernel}: Dispatches 1D thread blocks where each thread casts a direct solar beam from a sensor cell against triangle bounding boxes and primitives in parallel, outputting a bitmask array.
    \item \texttt{compute\_svf\_horizon\_kernel}: Evaluates 36 horizon elevation radials concurrently per sensor cell using warp-level parallel reduction.
\end{itemize}
As profiled in Section~VIII, this architecture executes certified incremental recomputation in $11.40\,\mathrm{ms}$, enabling dynamic interactive optimization.

\section{Active Surrogate Optimization}

\subsection{Parameter Space and Feasibility Screening}
Urban shade interventions are parameterized as 3D oriented rectangular canopies $\mathbf{p} = (x, y, L, W, H, \theta, \alpha_{\mathrm{panel}})$, where $(x, y)$ is the anchor coordinate, $L \in [3.0, 10.0]\,\mathrm{m}$ is length, $W \in [1.5, 4.0]\,\mathrm{m}$ is width, $H \in [3.5, 5.0]\,\mathrm{m}$ is underside clearance height, $\theta \in [0^\circ, 180^\circ]$ is orientation heading, and $\alpha_{\mathrm{panel}} \in [0.20, 0.80]$ is top-surface albedo.

To ensure physical feasibility, every candidate proposal passes a 4-stage geometric screening filter:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Corridor Containment:} The panel footprint must lie entirely within the 12-metre pedestrian walkway polygon.
    \item \textit{Building Collision Clearance:} Minimum 1.0-metre setback buffer from all existing building facades.
    \item \textit{Clearance Height Safety:} Minimum underside clearance $H \ge 3.5\,\mathrm{m}$ to allow emergency vehicle access.
    \item \textit{Maximum Structural Area:} Total panel area $A \le 30.0\,\mathrm{m}^2$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Intervention Objective with Overlap Penalty}
For multi-intervention layouts involving two panels $\mathbf{p}_1$ and $\mathbf{p}_2$, independent placement can lead to overlapping cast shadows, yielding diminishing returns. Solaraeus optimizes the composite objective:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{p}_1, \mathbf{p}_2) &= \sum_{x \in \mathcal{C}} \Delta T_{\mathrm{mrt}}(x) - \lambda_{\mathrm{area}}(A_1 + A_2) \\
    &\quad - \lambda_{\mathrm{cost}} C_{\mathrm{struct}} - \lambda_{\mathrm{overlap}} |\mathcal{S}_1 \cap \mathcal{S}_2|,
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}$ is the pedestrian corridor cell set, $\Delta T_{\mathrm{mrt}}(x) = T_{\mathrm{mrt}}^{\mathrm{base}}(x) - T_{\mathrm{mrt}}^{\mathrm{interv}}(x)$ is cooling improvement, $\lambda_{\mathrm{area}} = 0.005$ is area regularization, $\lambda_{\mathrm{cost}} = 0.0001$ models structural cost, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{overlap}} = 0.5$ penalizes redundant shadow overlap $|\mathcal{S}_1 \cap \mathcal{S}_2|$.

\subsection{Surrogate-Assisted Search}
To accelerate the optimization, Solaraeus trains a gradient-boosted surrogate regression model $\widehat{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{p})$ with variance uncertainty $\widehat{\sigma}^2(\mathbf{p})$ over evaluated designs. The search uses an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) acquisition strategy:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\mathrm{ucb}}(\mathbf{p}) = \widehat{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{p}) + \kappa \widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{p}),
    \label{eq:acquisition}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa = 1.96$ to balance exploitation of proven cooling zones with exploration of unvisited corridor segments.

\subsection{Documented Limitations and Scientific Integrity}
In strict adherence to scientific integrity, we explicitly document the following engineering boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Topographic Datum:} Church Street simulations utilize a certified flat-ground datum ($z = 0.0\,\mathrm{m}$). While synthetic terrain algorithms are verified in solver versions `2.1.0-cpu-terrain` and `2.1.0-gpu-terrain`, real-world micro-topographic claims remain bounded because measured engineering curb/gutter surveys are currently unavailable ($\mathrm{FABDEM\_REGIONAL\_REFERENCE\_ONLY}$).
    \item \textit{Street Tree Canopies:} Analytical Level~1 tree models (cylinder trunk and ellipsoid crown with Beer--Lambert shortwave extinction) are fully implemented and verified. However, tree geometries in Bengaluru remain photo-estimated ($\mathrm{TREE\_GEOMETRY\_PHOTO\_ESTIMATED\_ONLY}$) pending dedicated field surveys.
    \item \textit{Field Microclimate Calibration:} All presented figures represent modeled physics differences under fixed meteorological boundary forcing rather than in-situ pyranometer or globe thermometer measurements ($\mathrm{STAGE\_37\_FIELD\_DATA\_UNAVAILABLE}$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{Solaraeus}, an open-source, physics-based 3D urban microclimate digital twin and certified computational engine. By uniting Overture building footprints, Google Open Buildings machine-learning heights, and NOAA/NASA POWER atmospheric data into an automated Python and CUDA pipeline, Solaraeus computes 3D Steyn (1980) Sky View Factors across 36 azimuths, Möller--Trumbore ray-traced shadows, 6-flux Stefan--Boltzmann Mean Radiant Temperatures ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$), and Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) pedestrian heat stress. 

To eliminate the computational bottleneck of traditional microclimate solvers, we developed and proved a \textit{Certified Incremental Solver} with closed-form mathematical error bounds ($B_T(x) \le \varepsilon_T = 0.50\,\mathrm{K}$) grounded in solid-angle horizon truncation decay and concave Stefan--Boltzmann linearization. On an authentic digital twin of Church Street, Bengaluru ($28{,}120$ cells, 123 buildings), baseline simulations revealed severe pedestrian heat stress ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 45.81\degC$, $\mathrm{UTCI} = 36.49\degC$). An engineered overhead canopy ($\mathrm{BLR\_SHADE\_001}$) achieved $-12.62\,\mathrm{K}$ peak $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ cooling and $-3.10\,\mathrm{K}$ UTCI relief. The certified incremental solver recomputed only $72$ cells ($0.26\%$) while reusing $28{,}048$ cells ($99.74\%$), avoiding $99.74\%$ of ray evaluations with zero certificate violations and a maximum domain error of $0.0289\,\mathrm{K} \ll 0.50\,\mathrm{K}$. On an NVIDIA RTX 4050 GPU, the resident incremental kernel executed in $11.40\,\mathrm{ms}$ ($500.87\times$ speedup over full CPU recomputation). Furthermore, active surrogate optimization successfully discovered complementary multi-canopy layouts ($\mathrm{CAND\_4196\_SURR}$, $12.68\,\mathrm{K}$ peak cooling) with zero spatial overlap. Outputs are published directly to an interactive Three.js 3D WebGL digital twin. Solaraeus provides municipal engineers and urban designers with a certified, real-time computational platform for mitigating extreme heat in warming cities.
