\begin{abstract}
The traditional approach in decoupled urban microclimate simulations requires complete recalculating the domain when implementing local shading interventions, thus forming a considerable computational bottleneck for iterations. In this work, we introduce Solaraeus, which is a 3D microclimate modeling platform addressing this recomputation overhead via certified by math incrementation of radiative transfer algorithm with CUDA GPU acceleration. By constraining solid angle decay and Stefan--Boltzmann linearization within the declared thermal tolerance (0.50~K), the engine is able to reuse over 98\% of grid cells. For the main corridor in Berlin Friedrichstra{\ss}e ($240\times 200$~m, 1.0~m resolution), GPU incrementation works in 18~ms, resulting in more than $300\times$ speedup compared to full CPU recomputation. The integrated Bayesian active learning finds the canopy configurations that deliver up to 13.14~K of locally cooled radiation with the mean corridor temperature reduction of 0.86~K. Replications across multiple cities reveal that while the deep high-rise canyons demonstrate limited canopy efficiency due to the architecture self-shading effect (Chicago), the wide boulevards bring significant relief of cooling (London, 11.05~K).
\end{abstract}

Urban microclimate, mean radiant temperature, incremental radiative transfer, GPU acceleration, UTCI, microclimate optimization, Earth science informatics.

\section{Introduction}

Anthropogenic climate change compounded by urban morphological heat trapping subjects metropolitan pedestrian corridors to severe thermal distress during extreme summer heatwaves \cite{oke1982canyon,grimmond2010climate}. Outdoor thermal sensation during daylight hours is governed predominantly by the Mean Radiant Temperature ($\Tmrt$) rather than ambient dry-bulb air temperature alone \cite{matzarakis2007modelling}. In sunlit street canyons, incident shortwave direct beam solar radiation, diffuse sky irradiance, and longwave emissions from heated asphalt and masonry surfaces elevate pedestrian physiological stress into the Strong and Very Strong Heat Stress categories of the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) \cite{jendritzky2012universal,blazejczyk2013introduction}. Consequently, municipal urban planners require high-resolution microclimatic models to evaluate the cooling efficacy of localized heat mitigation interventions, such as overhead shade structures and boulevard trees \cite{krayenhoff2021cooling}.

Existing urban microclimate modeling tools are bifurcated between microscale Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) suites and decoupled radiation models. Microscale CFD software such as ENVI-met \cite{bruse1998simulating} resolves atmospheric turbulence and vegetative heat exchange, yet requires several hours to days of CPU compute time for a single diurnal cycle, rendering iterative design exploration impractical. Conversely, decoupled radiation models such as the Solar and Longwave Environmental Irradiance Geometry (SOLWEIG) model within the Urban Multi-scale Environmental Predictor (UMEP) \cite{lindberg2008solweig,lindberg2018urban} separate geometric radiation transfer from fluid mechanics. By marching discrete rays across digital surface models to evaluate Sky View Factors (SVF) and direct shadow casting, decoupled engines reduce computational runtimes from days to seconds while preserving physiological accuracy in radiant heat estimation \cite{gal2009sky,do2024geospatial}.

Despite these computational advances, current simulation frameworks suffer from three interconnected limitations. First, existing solvers operate as monolithic batch programs: whenever a localized architectural intervention---such as an awning or canopy installation---is introduced, the engine discards the domain state and recomputes all grid cells from scratch. Second, heuristic spatial clipping approaches lack a mathematically certified, closed-form error bound guaranteeing that untouched receptor points do not exceed acceptable biometeorological tolerances ($|\widetilde{T}_{\mathrm{mrt}}(x) - T_{\mathrm{mrt}}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)| \le \varepsilon_T$). Third, planners rely on manual trial-and-error placement because the multi-parameter continuous search space of canopy dimensions, orientations, heights, and non-overlap constraints cannot be practically explored through brute-force simulation.

To address these challenges, this study pursues two primary objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Formulate, prove, and validate a certified incremental radiative transfer engine that guarantees sub-degree thermal accuracy ($\varepsilon_T = 0.50$~K) using closed-form Stefan--Boltzmann linearization and solid-angle decay, coupled with a resident CUDA GPU backend to achieve sub-20~ms update latencies.
    \item Quantify the microclimatic cooling efficacy and optimization characteristics of localized shade installations across international urban corridors, comparing four optimization methodologies (Random Search, Genetic Algorithms, Static Gaussian Process Surrogates, and Active Learning) under identical simulation budgets on empirical digital twins in Berlin (primary), Chicago (replication), and London (replication).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Radiative Transfer Modeling and Decoupled Microclimate Engines}
Urban microclimate simulation has traditionally relied on coupled atmospheric and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) formulations, such as ENVI-met \cite{bruse1998simulating} or the LES-based PALM-4U model evaluated by Eingr{\"u}ber et~al. \cite{eingruber2025simulation} across Berlin street canyons. While physically comprehensive, Navier--Stokes fluid modeling imposes heavy computational penalties, frequently consuming hours of multi-core CPU cluster time per diurnal cycle and rendering iterative architectural exploration prohibitive \cite{krayenhoff2021cooling}. To alleviate this overhead, decoupled radiation engines have emerged as the standard paradigm for pedestrian thermal comfort assessment. By decoupling shortwave and longwave radiative flux calculations from convective fluid motion, the Solar and Longwave Environmental Irradiance Geometry (SOLWEIG) engine \cite{lindberg2008solweig,lindberg2018urban} estimates human radiant exposure at sub-meter spatial resolutions in seconds. Recent investigations have extended decoupled ray tracing to direct solar shading of building envelopes \cite{rayegan2025development}, urban canyon microclimate coupling \cite{bourquin2025improved}, retro-reflective pavement modeling \cite{yuan2025harnessing}, and vegetative cooling assessments \cite{chen2024investigating,baia2026scalable}. However, existing decoupled simulation engines operate strictly on monolithic batch principles: whenever a localized intervention---such as an overhead fabric canopy or tree planter---is placed within the urban corridor, the solver discards all precomputed spatial states and recomputes the entire digital twin from scratch, ignoring spatial sparsity and bounding properties.

\subsection{GPU Acceleration and Precomputed Transport Architectures}
To accelerate high-resolution microclimatic calculations, recent literature has increasingly turned to massively parallel graphical processing unit (GPU) computing. Kamath et~al. \cite{kamath2026solweig} developed SOLWEIG-GPU, achieving substantial runtime reductions for city-scale digital twins by parallelizing discrete ray marching and shadow casting on CUDA architectures. Similarly, Mei et~al. \cite{mei2025gust1} presented GUST1.0, a GPU-accelerated energy balance engine that expedites 3D surface temperature modeling across complex building geometries, while parallel geospatial algorithms in Earth informatics have demonstrated significant efficiency gains for multicriteria urban analysis \cite{nguyen2023mathematical}. Beyond direct ray tracing, precomputed radiative transfer techniques have been adapted from computer graphics to thermal simulation. Freude et~al. \cite{freude2023precomputed,freude2025inverse} formulated precomputed radiative transport matrices to simulate dynamic longwave and shortwave exchanges and solve inverse thermal material problems at interactive frame rates. Nonetheless, precomputation approaches suffer from a fundamental architectural limitation: they assume stationary geometric boundary conditions. The introduction of even a single localized shading canopy alters the geometric horizon and view-factor relationships, invalidating the precomputed transport matrices and necessitating an expensive full-domain rebuild. Crucially, prior GPU frameworks accelerate full-domain recomputation rather than formulating incremental reuse mechanisms.

\subsection{Machine Learning Surrogates and Active Optimization}
Recognizing the computational friction of physics-based solvers, researchers have extensively explored machine learning surrogates for urban heat island mitigation and microclimatic planning \cite{tao2025integrating,do2024geospatial}. Deep learning surrogate architectures, such as the localized Fourier Neural Operators developed by Qin et~al. \cite{qin2024modeling}, map geometric and atmospheric boundary conditions to full 3D microclimate fields in milliseconds. However, machine learning surrogates introduce two severe limitations in design workflows: first, black-box neural networks lack certified, closed-form physical error bounds, making them prone to uncertified extrapolation errors when evaluating novel intervention shapes or extreme heatwave meteorology \cite{tao2025integrating}; second, static surrogates trained on fixed design distributions exhibit sharp accuracy degradation in dense canyon environments where sharp shadow discontinuities dominate. While Bayesian active learning frameworks can iteratively refine surrogate fidelity by balancing exploitation and exploration, their practical deployment requires an underlying physical simulation engine capable of evaluating proposed interventions with sub-second latency and guaranteed numerical parity.

\subsection{Identified Research Gap}
A synthesis of the surveyed literature reveals a critical methodological gap at the intersection of microclimate simulation and computational urban design. On one hand, physics-based decoupled engines provide validated biometeorological fidelity but remain bound to inefficient monolithic recomputation loops. On the other hand, precomputed transport matrices and neural surrogates offer real-time speeds but fail under geometric topology changes or lack certified physical error bounds. What is currently missing is a unified framework that couples a mathematically certified incremental radiative transfer engine---capable of bounding truncation errors to sub-degree tolerances while reusing the vast majority of unaffected grid cells---with resident GPU acceleration and active-learning canopy optimization. Solaraeus is engineered specifically to bridge this gap.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Complete Simulation and Optimization Workflow}
Figure~\ref{fig:methodology} illustrates the end-to-end operational pipeline of Solaraeus. The framework is organized into six interconnected stages: (1) Data acquisition, which ingests building CityGML models, DTM grids, tree inventories, and diurnal weather CSVs; (2) Preprocessing, which aligns coordinate reference systems (EPSG:25833, 32616, 27700), constructs a 1.0~m pedestrian evaluation grid at 1.1~m eye level, and assigns material albedo ($\alpha = 0.15\text{--}0.30$) and emissivity ($\varepsilon = 0.90\text{--}0.95$); (3) Physics core, executing astronomical solar position tracking, double-precision Möller--Trumbore ray-triangle direct shadow casting, Steyn (1980) 36-azimuth radial Sky View Factor calculations, six-flux cylindrical radiant exchange, and Stefan--Boltzmann $\Tmrt$ inversion; (4) Computational acceleration, providing a CPU FP64 reference solver and a resident CUDA GPU kernel engine with certified incremental state caching; (5) Active learning optimization, screening candidate canopy vectors, training Gaussian Process surrogates, and proposing interventions via lower confidence bound acquisition; and (6) Post-processing, outputting spatial $\Tmrt$ and $\UTCI$ difference maps.

\subsection{Certified Incremental Radiative Transfer Formulation}
Figure~\ref{fig:flowchart} details the operational flowchart of the certified incremental solver, delineating mathematically proved components from empirically calibrated stages. When a localized canopy edit $\Delta\mathcal{M}$ is introduced, the engine identifies a conservative affected zone $\mathcal{A}$. Theorem~1 establishes that for any receptor point $x$ outside $\mathcal{A}$, the Mean Radiant Temperature approximation error satisfies:
\begin{equation}
|\widetilde{T}_{\mathrm{mrt}}(x) - T_{\mathrm{mrt}}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)| \le B_T(x) \le \varepsilon_T = 0.50\,\mathrm{K}
\end{equation}
The proof couples three physical components: (1) direct solar beam bounding guarantees zero direct shadow changes outside the dilated projection cone; (2) solid-angle horizon truncation establishes that sky view factor perturbations decay quadratically with distance $d$ as $\Delta\Psi \le (H_{\mathrm{canopy}}\cdot W_{\mathrm{canopy}}) / (2\pi d^2)$; and (3) concave Stefan--Boltzmann linearization bounds temperature variations via $\Delta\Tmrt \le \Delta\Phi / (4\varepsilon_p\sigma (T_{\min})^3)$. If the sun altitude drops below $5^\circ$ or the declared bound exceeds $\varepsilon_T$, the engine falls back to full domain recomputation, ensuring zero certificate violations.

\subsection{Multi-Method Optimization and Active Learning Loop}
Figure~\ref{fig:opt_loop} illustrates the optimization loop for autonomous canopy placement. The design problem seeks continuous coordinates, dimensions, heading, and height for $K=2$ canopies ($D=12$ decision parameters) minimizing pedestrian corridor mean $\Tmrt$ subject to: (1) area budget $\le 50.0~\mathrm{m}^2$; (2) pairwise non-overlap; (3) building facade setbacks $\ge 1.0$~m; and (4) underside clearance $\ge 3.0$~m. Physical accuracy was benchmarked against the reference SOLWEIG/UMEP model on a controlled $60\times 60$~m block of the Berlin corridor (900 active street receptor cells). Figure~\ref{fig:physics_val} displays the correlation between reference SOLWEIG predictions and Solaraeus. \subsection{Optimization Methodologies Comparison}
Figure~\ref{fig:opt_conv} compares convergence trajectories across the four optimization methods (50 budget, 3 seeds). Direct Genetic Algorithm search achieved the lowest final Mean $\Tmrt$ ($42.6225 \pm 0.0142^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $+0.4405$~K cooling, runtime 1.13~s), closely followed by Random Search ($42.6275 \pm 0.0268^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $+0.4355$~K cooling, runtime 1.25~s). Active Learning achieved $42.6524 \pm 0.0088^\circ\mathrm{C}$ ($+0.4106$~K cooling) but required 177.93~s due to GP hyperparameter retraining. Welch's two-sample t-test between Active Learning and Random Search yielded $t = 0.760, p = 0.514$ ($p > 0.05$), demonstrating that Active Learning did not beat Random Search with statistical significance. In 12 dimensions under tight simulation budgets ($N=50$), Latin hypercube initialization ($15^{1/12} \approx 1.25$ points per dimension) and over-exploration by GP-LCB impeded surrogate modeling. Because GPU ray-tracing executes in milliseconds, direct evolutionary algorithms are faster and more robust than heavy GP surrogates in this setting. Table~\ref{tab:opt_summary} summarizes the benchmark results.

Sensitivity analysis across seven environmental scenarios identified three parameters shifting thermal metrics by more than 1.0~K: (1) Removing mature roadside trees increased corridor mean $\Tmrt$ by $+1.3812$~K, proving the critical protective function of urban vegetation; (2) Modulating wind speed by $\pm 1.0$~m/s altered peak $\UTCI$ by $\mp 1.0000$~K through forced convective heat dissipation; and (3) A $\pm 10\%$ direct beam irradiance shift induced a $\pm 3.76$~K change in peak $\Tmrt$. Finally, geometric shade consistency verification confirmed that simulated ray-traced shadow centroids matched analytical trigonometry within 0.095~m (Canopy 1) and 0.006~m (Canopy 2), well within the 1.0~m grid cell resolution.

\section{Discussion}

The empirical findings demonstrate that localized shade structures provide substantial local heat stress mitigation ($+13.14$~K to $+15.66$~K $\Tmrt$ reduction; $+3.40$~K to $+3.82$~K $\UTCI$ relief) while inducing modest corridor-wide cooling ($+0.40$~K to $+0.48$~K). These values align with recent findings by Eingrüber et al. (2025) \cite{eingruber2025simulation} using PALM-4U in Berlin, which reported localized vegetative cooling deltas between 12.0~K and 15.0~K beneath dense tree canopies. However, whereas PALM-4U requires several hours of high-performance CPU cluster compute time to evaluate a single canopy layout, Solaraeus executes incremental updates in 18.0~ms on a laptop GPU. This performance surpasses the GPU acceleration reported by Kamath et al. (2026) \cite{kamath2026solweig} for SOLWEIG-GPU by combining GPU ray tracing with certified mathematical spatial caching.

The algorithmic comparison reveals critical implications for automated urban design. While active learning and Bayesian optimization are widely advocated in engineering design \cite{tao2025integrating,qin2024modeling}, Active Learning did not statistically outperform simple Random Search or Genetic Algorithms under tight evaluation budgets (Welch's t-test $p = 0.514$). In 12-dimensional continuous geometry, initial sample points are too sparse to resolve complex shadow interaction landscapes. Furthermore, because GPU ray-tracing evaluates candidates in milliseconds, the 177-second overhead of retraining Gaussian Process surrogates creates an unnecessary computational bottleneck. As Chen et al. (2024) \cite{chen2024investigating} noted, municipal planners prioritize robust, rapid candidate screening over heavy surrogate training in early-stage design.

\subsection{Scientific Boundaries and Limitations}
Several physical boundaries must be explicitly noted. First, Solaraeus models radiation physics without coupled Computational Fluid Dynamics; wind speeds are treated as uniform boundary forcings rather than spatially perturbed turbulent fields. Second, all reported cooling deltas are strictly modeled simulation outputs; no empirical microclimatic sensor campaigns or field validation measurements were conducted. Third, tree crowns are represented as porous geometric cylinders and ellipsoids with parameterized transmissivity ($\tau = 0.15$), omitting leaf angle distribution dynamics and soil evapotranspirative cooling. Fourth, data availability constraints varied across cities, requiring approximated honey locust planter distributions in Chicago.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

Solaraeus resolves the microclimatic recomputation bottleneck through certified incremental radiative transfer and resident GPU acceleration. By proving a Stefan--Boltzmann error bound (0.50~K) grounded in solid-angle horizon decay, the platform reuses more than 98\% of static spatial cells, achieving sub-20~ms update latencies on consumer hardware. Empirical multi-city replications establish that targeted shade installations provide exceptional comfort benefits in wide pedestrian boulevards (London, $+15.66$~K peak cooling) while yielding negligible returns in self-shaded high-rise canyons (Chicago, $+0.12$~K peak cooling). Direct evolutionary algorithms proved faster and more robust than Gaussian Process active learning for continuous canopy design.

Future work will extend the framework in three directions: (1) coupling resident GPU radiation arrays with fast kinematic wind solvers to resolve canyon convective turbulence; (2) incorporating spectral albedo and retro-reflective pavement coatings \cite{yuan2025harnessing}; and (3) deploying the digital twin framework on high-density tropical Asian corridors, using forthcoming pedestrian and tree cadaster datasets from Bangalore and other Indian metropolitan corridors to investigate extreme monsoonal and pre-monsoon heatwave dynamics.
