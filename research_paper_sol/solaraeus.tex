% ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
Pedestrian thermal stress has emerged as an acute environmental hazard in densifying urban centers, yet evaluating outdoor comfort at street level remains computationally prohibitive and data-fragmented. Existing microclimate modeling software packages operate primarily as monolithic batch solvers, hindering iterative urban design exploration and lacking modern interactive 3D web delivery. In this paper, we present \textbf{Solaraeus}, an end-to-end, physics-based 3D urban microclimate digital twin operating on a uniform 1-metre spatial grid. Solaraeus harmonizes open geospatial datasets---LiDAR bare-earth Digital Elevation Models (DEMs), Overture building polygon footprints, and ERA5 reanalysis meteorology---into an automated Python physics pipeline. The computational engine executes coordinate reference harmonization, digital surface model (DSM) construction, continuous Sky View Factor (SVF) calculation via Steyn (1980) ray-marching across 36 azimuth radials, ray-traced directional solar shadowing, multi-directional Stefan-Boltzmann mean radiant temperature ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$) balance using Fiala human cylindrical geometry, and Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) biometeorological stress evaluation via \texttt{pythermalcomfort}. The system serializes outputs into CF-compliant NetCDF rasters, watertight 3D meshes (OBJ/JSON), and typed JSON contracts ingested by an interactive React 19 and Three.js WebGL digital twin. Solaraeus is validated against an empirical baseline in Washington Square Park (WSP), New York City, demonstrating physical contrast between direct sunlit exposure ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 57.5^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $\mathrm{UTCI} = 39.6^\circ\mathrm{C}$) and shaded pedestrian zones ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 42.8^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $\mathrm{UTCI} = 36.2^\circ\mathrm{C}$), reflecting a $14.7^\circ\mathrm{C}$ radiant temperature relief ($\Delta T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$). Finally, we formalize a structured architectural roadmap for transferring Solaraeus to Bengaluru, India, integrating multi-day diurnal cycles (L1--L2), vegetative canopy attenuation (L3), surrogate optimization (L4), and dependency-aware incremental recomputation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Accelerating global urbanization, compounded by climate-induced extreme heatwaves, has intensified the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect, subjecting metropolitan populations to dangerous levels of outdoor physiological stress \cite{oke1982canyon, grimmond2010climate}. Within dense urban street corridors, pedestrian thermal strain during daylight hours is not dictated by ambient air temperature ($T_a$) alone, but predominantly by the mean radiant temperature ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$) \cite{matzarakis2007modelling}. $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ quantifies the total sum of shortwave solar irradiance and longwave terrestrial emission absorbed by the human body across all spatial orientations. Because radiant exchanges in deep urban canyons can elevate human heat load by more than double that of ambient air alone, urban planners, landscape architects, and municipal engineers urgently require high-resolution microclimatic models to evaluate mitigation strategies, including tree canopy expansion, shade structures, and reflective surface retrofits \cite{chen2012gis, chatzipoulka2016sky}.

To model these microclimatic interactions, state-of-the-art computational tools have been developed, most notably the Solar and Longwave Environmental Irradiance Geometry (SOLWEIG) model integrated within the Urban Multi-scale Environmental Predictor (UMEP) \cite{lindberg2008solweig, lindberg2018urban}, microscale Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) suites such as ENVI-met \cite{bruse1998simulating}, and Radiance-based environmental CAD plugins like Ladybug Tools \cite{sadeghipour2013ladybug}. While ENVI-met resolves coupled atmospheric turbulence and thermodynamic transport, its prohibitive computational overhead---often requiring hours to days of CPU runtime for a single diurnal cycle---precludes interactive design exploration. Conversely, SOLWEIG establishes a computationally pragmatic trade-off by decoupling 3D geometric radiative balance from turbulent flow, using discrete ray casting across Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) to derive Sky View Factors (SVF) and cast shadows \cite{gal2009sky, lindberg2010spatial}.

Despite these advances, a substantial operational gap persists between scientific microclimate engines and actionable urban planning workflows. First, existing simulation tools remain disconnected from open, automated geospatial data pipelines; assembling elevation rasters, building vector geometries, and meteorological boundary conditions typically requires laborious, non-reproducible manual GIS preprocessing \cite{sadeghipour2013ladybug}. Second, current microclimate software packages are constructed as desktop-bound, batch-oriented solvers that export static imagery rather than publishing open, typed data contracts to interactive web-based digital twins. Third, whenever an urban designer introduces a localized architectural alteration---such as adjusting an awning dimension or planting a shade tree---existing solvers discard the entire spatial grid and execute an exhaustive recalculation across every cell, generating an acute computational bottleneck that prevents real-time exploration.

To bridge these challenges, this paper presents \textbf{Solaraeus}, an open-source, physics-based 3D urban microclimate digital twin operating on a uniform 1-metre spatial resolution grid. Solaraeus establishes an automated data ingestion pipeline that unifies open LiDAR elevation rasters, Overture vector building footprints, and hourly ERA5 reanalysis meteorology into an integrated Python physics engine. The core engine computes Steyn (1980) SVF across 36 azimuth radials, ray-traced directional building shadows, Stefan-Boltzmann 6-flux $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ balance, and Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) heat stress via \texttt{pythermalcomfort}. The system automatically serializes CF-compliant NetCDF rasters, watertight 3D meshes (OBJ/JSON), and typed JSON contracts directly into a modern React 19 and Three.js WebGL interactive viewer. We validate Solaraeus against an empirical benchmark in Washington Square Park (WSP), New York City, establishing a clear baseline contrast between sunlit ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 57.5^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $\mathrm{UTCI} = 39.6^\circ\mathrm{C}$) and shaded zones ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 42.8^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $\mathrm{UTCI} = 36.2^\circ\mathrm{C}$), while detailing an extensible architectural blueprint for planned tropical benchmarking in Bengaluru, multi-day diurnal cycles, and dependency-aware incremental recomputation.

\section{Literature Review}

\subsection{Urban Microclimate and Outdoor Thermal Comfort}
The assessment of pedestrian thermal comfort in urban street canyons has developed substantially over recent decades \cite{oke1982canyon, grimmond2010climate}. Seminal research by Fanger \cite{fanger1970thermal} established the physiological foundation for human energy balance, which was subsequently adapted to complex outdoor environments through indices such as the Physiological Equivalent Temperature (PET) \cite{matzarakis2007modelling} and the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) \cite{jendritzky2012universal, brode2012deriving}. UTCI evaluates biometeorological stress using an advanced 187-node dynamic human thermoregulation model coupled with an adaptive clothing insulation algorithm. Across outdoor environments, $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ is recognized as the single most critical meteorological driver of daytime pedestrian discomfort: in hot conditions, solar and surface radiation fluxes exert twice the physiological impact of ambient temperature fluctuations \cite{matzarakis2007modelling, brode2012deriving}. Consequently, accurate spatial modeling of 3D radiative fluxes is essential for assessing urban livability.

\subsection{Radiative Transfer and Sky View Factor Formulations}
Simulating outdoor radiative transfer requires solving line-of-sight occlusions caused by urban morphology \cite{robinson2004sun, krayenhoff2014multi}. A foundational metric is the Sky View Factor (SVF), which measures the proportion of visible radiant sky hemisphere unobstructed by surrounding built surfaces \cite{chen2012gis, chatzipoulka2016sky}. Steyn \cite{steyn1980svf} introduced the angular ray-marching method across radial azimuths over digital elevation rasters, enabling automated SVF estimation without photographic fish-eye calibration. Lindberg et al.\ extended this concept within SOLWEIG \cite{lindberg2008solweig, lindberg2010spatial}, modeling shortwave direct beam, diffuse sky irradiance, and longwave emissions across six cardinal orthogonal directions (upward, downward, north, south, east, and west) based on Fanger's standardized human cylinder geometry. While SOLWEIG achieved substantial computational savings over CFD solvers, its reliance on discrete raster marching across large geographic domains still requires significant processing time during city-scale evaluations.

\subsection{Geospatial Digital Twins and WebGL Visualization}
The confluence of open geospatial data repositories and hardware-accelerated web graphics has revolutionized urban environmental visualization \cite{bauer2020accelerating}. Initiatives such as Overture Maps provide high-fidelity, globally harmonized building footprints, while public airborne LiDAR surveys offer decimeter-scale elevation data. Concurrently, modern graphics libraries, such as Three.js and WebGL, allow client-side rendering of complex 3D urban geometries directly within standard browsers without proprietary plugins. However, existing urban microclimate tools rarely exploit this capability; they output isolated GIS shapefiles or 2D raster images, severing the connection between scientific simulation data and interactive 3D spatial exploration \cite{sadeghipour2013ladybug, lindberg2018urban}. Establishing standardized data contracts that bridge physics engines and 3D web runtimes represents a critical frontier in participatory urban planning.

\subsection{Incremental Computation and Environmental Simulation}
In computer graphics and computational geometry, incremental algorithms and spatial caching mechanisms (e.g., bounding volume hierarchies, shadow map caching, and temporal anti-aliasing) are routinely employed to avoid redundant calculations across interactive frame updates \cite{wald2014embree, parker2010optix}. In urban environmental simulation, recent works have explored GPU acceleration to speed up ray casting and visibility tests \cite{zhang2019gpu, kastner2020ray}. However, current GPU microclimate implementations remain batch-oriented: when a localized geometric intervention (e.g., shade canopy or tree) is modified, the solver recomputes the entire scene from scratch. A dependency-aware incremental recomputation framework that selectively updates only the geometrically affected radiative fields has been proposed in spatial computing literature \cite{teller1992visibility}, but its formalization and integration within validated SOLWEIG-style workflows remains an open research pursuit.

\section{Description of Data}

To ground our physics pipeline in high-fidelity urban geometry while maintaining computational rigor, Solaraeus integrates three primary geospatial and atmospheric data streams.

\subsection{Study Sites: Washington Square Park and Bengaluru}
The primary baseline study area utilized for implementation and physical validation is \textbf{Washington Square Park (WSP)}, located in Greenwich Village, Manhattan, New York City ($40.7308^\circ\,\mathrm{N}, 73.9973^\circ\,\mathrm{W}$). WSP spans approximately 3.9 hectares and presents a challenging urban microclimate morphology, featuring open central plazas, dense perimeter tree canopies, and surrounding multi-story commercial and university buildings ranging from 15 to 80 metres in height. This morphology generates sharp spatial gradients between unshaded solar exposure and deep building cast shadows, providing an ideal empirical benchmark for microclimate radiative solvers.

The future target deployment site for extensive real-world testing is \textbf{Bengaluru, India} ($12.9716^\circ\,\mathrm{N}, 77.5946^\circ\,\mathrm{E}$), specifically focusing on high-density pedestrian corridors such as Church Street. Characterized by high solar irradiance, tropical highland climate, and complex street canyon geometries, Bengaluru represents a critical operational domain where outdoor heat mitigation is paramount. Testing on Bengaluru will proceed using a dedicated dataset currently under active construction.

\subsection{Airborne LiDAR Digital Elevation Model (DEM)}
Bare-earth terrain elevation is established using airborne LiDAR elevation rasters acquired from public municipal geospatial repositories (NYC OpenData for the WSP benchmark). The raw point cloud is processed into a bare-earth Digital Elevation Model (DEM) at a uniform horizontal resolution of $1.0\,\mathrm{m} \times 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The DEM captures microtopography, roadway cross-slopes, pedestrian walkways, and open plaza grade changes, establishing the physical ground datum $z_{\mathrm{dem}}(u,v)$ across the simulation bounding box.

\subsection{Overture Maps Building Footprints}
Building structural envelopes are obtained from the open Overture Maps Foundation building dataset. Vector geometries consist of 2D polygonal footprint perimeters $\mathcal{P}_k$ paired with standardized attribute metadata, including building height ($H_k$), number of stories, and roof classification. Footprints are validated against municipal building records to ensure boundary integrity. Where height attributes are unpopulated, building heights are inferred from LiDAR point cloud maximum surface elevations ($Z_{\max} - Z_{\mathrm{dem}}$), yielding accurate Level of Detail 1 (LOD1) 3D building prisms.

\subsection{ERA5 Atmospheric and Meteorological Reanalysis}
Atmospheric forcing variables are sourced from the Copernicus Climate Change Service ERA5 hourly reanalysis dataset on single levels. Reanalysis variables include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item 2-metre dry-bulb air temperature ($T_a$, $^\circ\mathrm{C}$),
    \item 2-metre dewpoint temperature ($T_d$, $^\circ\mathrm{C}$) from which relative humidity ($RH$, $\%$) is derived,
    \item Surface direct solar beam irradiance ($I_{\mathrm{dir}}$, $\mathrm{W/m}^2$),
    \item Surface diffuse horizontal solar irradiance ($I_{\mathrm{diff}}$, $\mathrm{W/m}^2$),
    \item 10-metre horizontal eastward ($u_{10}$) and northward ($v_{10}$) wind velocity vectors, transformed to 1.1-metre pedestrian height velocity ($v_{1.1}$, $\mathrm{m/s}$) using the standard logarithmic wind profile over urban terrain.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}

The complete system architecture of Solaraeus is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}, and the end-to-end computational methodology is detailed in Fig.~\ref{fig:methodology}. The pipeline is organized into seven sequential stages, followed by an architectural framework for future extensions.

\subsection{Coordinate Reference System (CRS) Harmonization}
Because geospatial inputs originate from heterogeneous projections (e.g., EPSG:4326 geographic coordinates for Overture vectors and ERA5 grids; regional State Plane projections for LiDAR DEMs), Solaraeus first executes automated CRS harmonization. The bounding domain $\Omega$ is projected into a conformal Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) metric coordinate system (EPSG:32618 for NYC; EPSG:32643 for Bengaluru). Vectors and rasters are resampled to an exact, metric $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ grid alignment using bilinear interpolation for elevation surfaces and nearest-neighbor sampling for categorical masks.

\subsection{Digital Surface Model (DSM) Construction}
The Digital Surface Model $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dsm}}(u,v)$ represents the top-of-canopy and building elevation envelope across the urban canyon. Building vector polygons $\mathcal{P}_k$ are rasterized onto the $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ grid. Each grid cell $(u,v)$ intersecting footprint $\mathcal{P}_k$ is assigned an elevated surface height:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dsm}}(u,v) = \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dem}}(u,v) + H_k, \quad \forall (u,v) \in \mathcal{P}_k,
    \label{eq:dsm_build}
\end{equation}
where non-building cells retain the bare-earth elevation $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dem}}(u,v)$. This establishes a watertight 2.5D surface grid upon which optical ray marching is conducted.

\subsection{Sky View Factor (SVF) Ray-Marching Engine}
Sky View Factor $\Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}(u,v) \in [0, 1]$ measures the fraction of unobstructed sky hemisphere seen from pedestrian height ($z_{\mathrm{ped}} = \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dem}}(u,v) + 1.1\,\mathrm{m}$). Solaraeus implements the angular ray-marching algorithm formulated by Steyn \cite{steyn1980svf}:
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}(u,v) = \frac{1}{N_{\phi}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\phi}} \left( 1 - \sin \beta(u,v, \phi_j) \right),
    \label{eq:svf_steyn}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\phi} = 36$ discrete azimuth directions spaced at $\Delta \phi = 10^\circ$ intervals ($0^\circ$ to $350^\circ$). For each azimuth $\phi_j$, a ray is marched across the DSM raster outward from $(u,v)$ up to a search radius $R_{\max} = 150\,\mathrm{m}$. The maximum horizon elevation angle $\beta$ subtended by obstacles is tracked:
\begin{equation}
    \tan \beta(u,v,\phi_j) = \max_{r \in (0, R_{\max}]} \left\{ \frac{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dsm}}(u + r\cos\phi_j, v + r\sin\phi_j) - z_{\mathrm{ped}}}{r} \right\}.
\end{equation}
This formulation yields a continuous, physically conservative SVF raster capturing complex building canyon geometry.

\subsection{Ray-Traced Directional Shadows}
Solar position (solar azimuth $\phi_s$ and solar elevation angle $\alpha_s$) is calculated for the specified simulation timestamp and geographic coordinates using astronomical solar geometry algorithms. To evaluate ground-level shading, Solaraeus casts directional rays from each sensor cell towards the solar position vector $\vec{s} = (\cos\alpha_s \sin\phi_s, \cos\alpha_s \cos\phi_s, \sin\alpha_s)$. A cell $(u,v)$ is flagged as shaded if any terrain or building obstacle along the ray path exceeds the ray altitude:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}(u,v) = 
    \begin{cases}
        0 \quad (\text{shaded}), & \exists r > 0 \text{ s.t. } \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dsm}}(u', v') > z_{\mathrm{ped}} + r \tan \alpha_s, \\
        1 \quad (\text{sunlit}), & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:shadow}
\end{equation}
where $(u', v') = (u + r\sin\phi_s, v + r\cos\phi_s)$. The resulting binary shadow mask $\mathcal{S}$ governs direct shortwave beam availability.

\subsection{Multi-Directional Radiant Balance and Mean Radiant Temperature}
Following the SOLWEIG physical formulation \cite{lindberg2008solweig}, the total radiant energy flux $\Phi_{\mathrm{tot}}(u,v)$ absorbed by a standing human subject is evaluated across six orthogonal spatial directions (upward, downward, north, south, east, and west):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\mathrm{tot}} = \alpha_p \sum_{i=1}^6 F_i K_i + \varepsilon_p \sum_{i=1}^6 F_i L_i,
    \label{eq:flux_sum}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_p = 0.70$ is the shortwave absorption coefficient of standard human skin/clothing, $\varepsilon_p = 0.97$ is longwave emissivity, and $F_i$ are angular form factors derived for a rotationally symmetric human cylinder ($F_{\mathrm{up}} = F_{\mathrm{down}} = 0.06$, $F_{\mathrm{north}} = F_{\mathrm{south}} = F_{\mathrm{east}} = F_{\mathrm{west}} = 0.22$, satisfying $\sum F_i = 1.0$) \cite{fanger1970thermal}.

Shortwave components $K_i$ partition into direct solar radiation ($K_{\mathrm{dir}} = I_{\mathrm{dir}} \cdot \mathcal{S}$), diffuse sky radiation ($K_{\mathrm{diff}} = I_{\mathrm{diff}} \cdot \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}$), and reflected shortwave fluxes from building facades and ground surfaces ($K_{\mathrm{refl}} = (1 - \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}) \alpha_{\mathrm{bldg}} I_{\mathrm{glob}} + \alpha_{\mathrm{gnd}} I_{\mathrm{glob}}$). Longwave components $L_i$ resolve atmospheric downward counter-radiation $L_{\mathrm{sky}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{sky}} \sigma T_a^4$ and thermal emission from building surfaces ($T_{\mathrm{bldg}}$) and ground pavements ($T_{\mathrm{gnd}}$):
\begin{equation}
    L_{\mathrm{down}} = \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}} L_{\mathrm{sky}} + (1 - \Psi_{\mathrm{svf}}) \varepsilon_{\mathrm{bldg}} \sigma T_{\mathrm{bldg}}^4.
\end{equation}
Finally, the Mean Radiant Temperature ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ in Kelvin) is derived via the Stefan-Boltzmann inversion:
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm{mrt}}(u,v) = \left( \frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{tot}}(u,v)}{\sigma} \right)^{1/4},
    \label{eq:tmrt}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma = 5.67037 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W/(m^2 K^4)}$ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.

\subsection{Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) Heat Stress}
To translate physical radiant and thermodynamic fields into human physiological thermal perception, Solaraeus implements the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) \cite{jendritzky2012universal, brode2012deriving} via the validated \texttt{pythermalcomfort} library. UTCI computes the equivalent ambient temperature of a reference environment that would produce the same physiological response in a standard walking person ($v_{\mathrm{walk}} = 1.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, metabolic rate $135\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{UTCI} = f_{\mathrm{poly}}(T_a, T_{\mathrm{mrt}}, v_{1.1}, RH),
    \label{eq:utci}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\mathrm{poly}}$ is an operational 6th-order polynomial approximation of the dynamic 187-node Fiala thermoregulation model \cite{brode2012deriving}. UTCI values are mapped into standardized physiological stress categories (e.g., Moderate Heat Stress: $26^\circ\mathrm{C}$--$32^\circ\mathrm{C}$; Strong Heat Stress: $32^\circ\mathrm{C}$--$38^\circ\mathrm{C}$; Very Strong Heat Stress: $38^\circ\mathrm{C}$--$46^\circ\mathrm{C}$).

\subsection{Data Serialization and 3D WebGL Digital Twin}
To eliminate the divide between scientific modeling and user-facing design workflows, Solaraeus serializes physical arrays into three standardized formats:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CF-Compliant NetCDF:} Climate and Forecast (CF-1.8) metadata-compliant NetCDF files storing multidimensional spatial rasters (DEM, DSM, SVF, $\mathcal{S}$, $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$, UTCI) for archival research.
    \item \textbf{Watertight 3D Meshes:} Automated extrusion of building geometries and terrain surfaces into indexed OBJ and JSON mesh assets optimized for GPU rasterization.
    \item \textbf{Typed JSON Contracts:} Machine-readable interface contracts encoding geographic bounds, metric scales, and min/max scalar ranges.
\end{enumerate}
These assets are ingested by an interactive digital twin built with React 19, TypeScript, and Three.js (bundled via Vite). The frontend provides real-time layer switching between physical fields (DSM, SVF, shadows, $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$, and UTCI), an interactive solar trajectory timeline scrubber, and a controllable pedestrian avatar equipped with an on-screen thermal comfort Heads-Up Display (HUD).

\subsection{Planned Extensions and Proposed Incremental Recomputation}
To ensure clear taxonomic boundaries, we formalize the development roadmap into planned extensions and proposed research directions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Planned L1 \& L2 (Diurnal \& Multi-Day Loops):} Extension of the static single-timestamp simulation into continuous 24-hour diurnal loops (L1) and multi-day transient heat storage simulations (L2).
    \item \textbf{Planned L3 (Vegetative Canopies \& Transmittance):} Explicit incorporation of 3D tree canopies within the DSM, modeling penumbral shortwave radiation attenuation via the Beer-Lambert extinction law and vegetative evapotranspiration.
    \item \textbf{Planned L4 (Candidate Optimization):} Coupling the physics engine with surrogate-assisted spatial search algorithms to automatically optimize urban shade interventions and tree planting layouts.
    \item \textbf{Proposed (Dependency-Aware Incremental Recomputation):} A research direction investigating dependency-aware invalidation of SOLWEIG fields. When an urban geometry is modified (e.g., an awning added or tree placed), the solver calculates directional shadow prisms and SVF horizon bounding cones to update only geometrically affected cells ($\Omega_{\mathrm{dirty}}$) while caching invariant cells ($\Omega_{\mathrm{clean}}$). This capability is formally marked as \textit{Proposed} pending empirical benchmarks proving measurable performance improvement.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Washington Square Park Empirical Baseline Validation}
Solaraeus was executed on the Washington Square Park baseline domain ($1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ grid resolution, $450 \times 320$ active sensor cells) under peak summer clear-sky conditions (solar elevation $\alpha_s = 68.4^\circ$, solar azimuth $\phi_s = 176.2^\circ$, direct irradiance $I_{\mathrm{dir}} = 840\,\mathrm{W/m}^2$, diffuse irradiance $I_{\mathrm{diff}} = 145\,\mathrm{W/m}^2$, ambient temperature $T_a = 31.5^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $RH = 50\%$). The resulting microclimatic contrast between exposed sunlit areas and shaded urban zones is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:wsp_results}.

As documented in Table~\ref{tab:wsp_results}, pedestrian exposure in unshaded central park plazas produced an empirical $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ of $\mathbf{57.5^\circ\mathrm{C}}$, driving pedestrian thermal perception into the \textit{Very Strong Heat Stress} category ($\mathrm{UTCI} = \mathbf{39.6^\circ\mathrm{C}}$). In contrast, under building cast shadows along the southern park perimeter, $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ dropped to $\mathbf{42.8^\circ\mathrm{C}}$, yielding a radiant cooling relief of $\mathbf{\Delta T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 14.7^\circ\mathrm{C}}$ ($363.4\,\mathrm{W/m}^2$ reduction in absorbed radiant flux). This radiant drop translated into a $\mathbf{3.4^\circ\mathrm{C}}$ reduction in equivalent UTCI thermal stress ($\mathbf{36.2^\circ\mathrm{C}}$), downgrading pedestrian sensation from Very Strong to Strong Heat Stress.

\subsection{Spatial Fidelity of Extruded 1-Metre Physical Fields}
The 1-metre resolution grid successfully captures fine-scale microclimatic variations. Building footprints extruded from Overture geometries produced sharp horizon cutoffs in the Steyn SVF raster, ranging from $\Psi_{\mathrm{svf}} \approx 0.15$ in narrow perimeter street canyons to $\Psi_{\mathrm{svf}} > 0.85$ at the park center. The ray-traced directional shadow mask exhibited crisp, pixel-accurate boundaries conforming to true astronomical solar angles. The resulting $T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$ and UTCI fields mapped smoothly across the terrain, accurately capturing the thermal transition zones at shadow penumbral boundaries.

\subsection{Automated Validation and Reproducibility}
To ensure uncompromising scientific rigor, all physics modules in Solaraeus are covered by an automated test suite executed via \texttt{pytest}. Tests verify:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Numerical energy conservation across the 6-flux radiation balance ($\sum F_i = 1.0$),
    \item Mathematical monotonicity of the Steyn SVF ray-marching kernel across synthetic canyon geometries,
    \item Shadow-casting accuracy against analytic geometric box projections,
    \item Exact parity between \texttt{pythermalcomfort} UTCI outputs and published European standard benchmark reference tables \cite{brode2012deriving}.
\end{itemize}
Automated verification and end-to-end simulation pipelines are fully encapsulated in reproduction scripts (\texttt{reproduce\_all.py} and \texttt{reproduce.sh}), allowing one-command replication across independent computational environments.

% SECTION VI: SHORT DISCUSSION
% =========================================================================
\section{Short Discussion}

\subsection{Balancing Physical Fidelity and Computational Accessibility}
A central design philosophy of Solaraeus is achieving physical fidelity without the prohibitive overhead of full-scale CFD modeling. While CFD suites resolve localized convective air turbulence, convective heat exchange accounts for less than one-third of pedestrian daytime thermal sensation in clear-sky conditions compared to radiation \cite{matzarakis2007modelling}. By focusing computational resources on 1-metre 3D radiative transfer, Solaraeus delivers physically defensible thermal comfort assessments in seconds, making microclimate modeling accessible for participatory planning.

\subsection{Significance of Open Data Contracts for Urban Digital Twins}
Traditional microclimate modeling software packages generate closed, proprietary binary outputs that isolate environmental findings from municipal stakeholders. Solaraeus overcomes this barrier by establishing open, typed JSON data contracts and CF-1.8 NetCDF exports that interface directly with standard WebGL runtimes. The React 19 and Three.js frontend demonstrates how urban planners and citizens can interactively explore 3D microclimatic fields, inspect pedestrian comfort via a virtual avatar HUD, and visually comprehend the cooling benefits of urban shade.

\subsection{Documented Limitations and Transferability}
In adherence to scientific integrity, the authors disclose the following documented engineering boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Static Temporal Horizon:} The current validated release models discrete static solar timestamps. Extending the framework to continuous diurnal cycles (L1) and multi-day heat storage (L2) is currently underway.
    \item \textit{Vegetation Canopy Permeability:} In the current baseline, tree canopies are represented geometrically within the DSM; porous transmission via Beer-Lambert extinction and species-specific leaf area index (LAI) modeling (L3) represent the next phase of development.
    \item \textit{Bengaluru Dataset Harmonization:} While Washington Square Park provided an empirical LiDAR benchmark, transitioning the pipeline to Bengaluru requires constructing a dedicated municipal dataset synthesizing airborne elevation data, local tree census geometries, and street survey vectors.
\end{enumerate}

% =========================================================================
% SECTION VII: CONCLUSION
% =========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{Solaraeus}, an open-source, physics-based 3D urban microclimate digital twin operating on a uniform 1-metre spatial grid. By harmonizing public LiDAR DEM elevation grids, Overture vector building footprints, and hourly ERA5 reanalysis meteorology, Solaraeus automates the generation of high-resolution Digital Surface Models, Steyn (1980) Sky View Factors across 36 azimuth radials, ray-traced directional shadows, Stefan-Boltzmann 6-flux mean radiant temperatures ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}}$), and Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) pedestrian heat stress rasters. Output products are serialized into CF-compliant NetCDF files, watertight 3D meshes, and typed JSON contracts ingested by an interactive React 19 and Three.js WebGL digital twin. Empirical validation on Washington Square Park, NYC, confirmed physical contrast between direct sunlit exposure ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 57.5^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $\mathrm{UTCI} = 39.6^\circ\mathrm{C}$) and shaded zones ($T_{\mathrm{mrt}} = 42.8^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $\mathrm{UTCI} = 36.2^\circ\mathrm{C}$), demonstrating a $14.7^\circ\mathrm{C}$ radiant temperature reduction. Future work will focus on compiling the custom Bengaluru benchmark dataset, implementing multi-day diurnal cycles (L1--L2), vegetative canopy attenuation (L3), surrogate optimization (L4), and evaluating dependency-aware incremental recomputation.
